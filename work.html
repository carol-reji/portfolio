PROJECT 04UT SOLAR VEHICLES TEAMSTRUCTURES

Pedal box topology optimisation

The pedal box was over-built because nobody had ever run the numbers on it — it just had to feel solid. I set up the load case properly (panic braking input at the pedal face, worst-case driver), ran topology optimisation, and cut material out of everywhere the solver said was doing nothing.

Then I checked it the boring way: hand calc on the critical section, and a mesh convergence study so the FoS wasn't an artefact of a coarse mesh.

PROJECT 05UT SOLAR VEHICLES TEAMCOMPOSITES

Composite aeroshell

Fiberglass layup, mold prep and finishing on the bottom shell — the part that had to be light, stiff and, unglamorously, actually come out of the mold. This is where I learned composites with my hands before I ever wrote a ply schedule for flight hardware.

The shell went on the car that raced — the first UT solar car to compete in over a decade.

PROJECT 06UT SOLAR VEHICLES TEAMTHERMAL

Battery pack cooling

A sealed pack in a Texas summer inside a body panel that is, by design, a greenhouse. I worked the heat-transfer path from cell to ambient — conduction into the pack structure, then forced convection — and sized the airflow the pack needed to stay inside cell temperature limits at race duty cycle.

The interesting constraint was not thermal at all: every vent is a hole in a waterproof enclosure and a drag penalty on a solar car.

Send me the temperature numbers and duty cycle and this becomes a much stronger entry.

PROJECT 07DESIGN / BUILD / FLYAERO STRUCTURES

DBF wing composites

Composite wing structure for a competition aircraft where every gram is scored. Layup and bagging on thin skins, where the failure modes are wrinkles and resin-starved corners rather than anything a stress plot would show you.

Same skill set as the Firefly work at 1/1000th the part cost — which is exactly why it was the right place to learn it.

PROJECT 08UT RESEARCHEM SIMULATION

Plasma reactor field study

Electromagnetic simulation of a plasma reactor geometry in HFSS — building the model, setting boundary conditions and ports, and reading the field distribution to understand where energy was actually going.

Included because it is the one project here that is purely analytical. Mechanical engineers who can set up a credible simulation and then argue with its results are more useful than ones who can only do one of those.
