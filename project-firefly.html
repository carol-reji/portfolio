Blue Ghost lunar lander composite tooling

I led the full manufacturing cycle for a Blue Ghost lunar lander part — CAD, tolerance stack-up and composite DFM through CNC-to-floor production. I improved structural detooler flatness tolerance from 0.067″ to 0.007″ (×9.6 tighter), and cut 4+ weeks off the tool launch timeline by working live-floor issues with technicians instead of over email.

01

The problem I inherited

A structural beam on the Blue Ghost lunar lander was being laid up over a hard tool and coming off with two linked defects: fiber bridging in the internal radii, and post-cure flatness measuring around 0.067″ against a spec that wanted an order of magnitude less. Every part needed rework, and every rework was a nonconformance to write and disposition.

The second problem was physical. The cured part weighs 6,500 lbf and there was no safe, repeatable way to get it off the tool. The existing method put point loads into a part whose whole job is to be flat, which meant the detooling step was itself generating the defect the inspection was catching.

04

The sequence, dead ends included

A
Measured before theorising
Pulled the CMM data on the existing parts and mapped where the flatness was going. The error was not random — it clustered at the radii, which pointed at bridging rather than cure shrinkage.
B
Tried the cheap fix first — it failed
More vacuum and a longer hold. It compacted the flats and did nothing for the radii, because bridging is a geometry problem, not a pressure problem.
C
Rewrote the ply book
Tapered the inner plies so no single ply had to make the full radius, staggered the overlaps so joints never stacked, and inserted targeted intermediate debulks at the plies that were doing the bridging.
D
Attacked detooling as its own design problem
Specified a crane-operable, oven-safe cart that supports the beam along its length instead of at points, so the part comes off the tool carrying its own weight the way it will in service.
E
Wrote it down so it outlived the internship
Turned the whole thing into a work instruction with photos and inspection points, then walked two technicians through it on a real part.
05

Validation

CMM inspection on the parts run under the new ply book and detooling method. Flatness came in at 0.007″ against the 0.067″ baseline, and the radii cleared bridging inspection. Because the process was documented rather than demonstrated, the improvement held on parts run after I left.

What I'd do differently

I spent a week on the vacuum-and-hold theory before I looked hard at the CMM clustering. The data was sitting there the whole time and would have pointed me at bridging on day one. Next time the inspection data gets read before I form an opinion, not after.
