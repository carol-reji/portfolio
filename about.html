PERSONNEL FILE

Fiberglass in a student shop to flight hardware in three years

I am a mechanical engineering student at UT Austin, graduating in 2027 on the design and manufacturing track. I started by laying up fiberglass for a solar car with no idea what a ply schedule was. Last summer I owned the manufacturing process and tooling for a structural beam on a lunar lander.

The thread through all of it is that I like the part of engineering where the drawing meets the shop floor. Fixtures. Ply books. Tolerance stacks. Detooling methods. The work that decides whether a good design becomes a good part or a stack of nonconformances — and the work that, done properly, keeps working after you leave.

I want to do manufacturing and process engineering — or mechanical design — at a launch or spacecraft company. I graduate in May 2027 and I am looking for a full-time role starting then.

Mission log

2023
First layup, UT Solar Vehicles Team
Squeegee, roller, and no theory. Learned composites as a physical craft first — which is why the ply schedules I write now assume a human being has to reach into that radius.
2024
Aeroshell on the car that raced
First UT solar car to compete in over a decade — 22.05 miles. Also the pedal box, the battery cooling path, and a DBF wing on the side.
2025
Firefly Aerospace — composites manufacturing
Led the full manufacturing cycle for a Blue Ghost lunar lander part. Structural detooler flatness 0.067″ → 0.007″, 4+ weeks off the tool launch, work instructions and MRRs on 200+ composite parts. Wrote the documentation that kept it working after I left.
2025—
Longhorn Baja — steering lead
Took a subsystem the team had never designed and gave it CAD, calcs, a trade study and a BOM. Building it now.
2026
Tesla — interior packaging
Human factors and ergonomics: door clearance and interior compartment concept, fit envelopes across three vehicle lines, on an allufix seat jig I designed and cut myself.

The part that isn't on a résumé
