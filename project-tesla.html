Occupant fit envelopes across three vehicle lines

Launched a door clearance and interior compartment concept by analysing 200+ product geometries, running market research with 60 participants, and computing 90%, 95% and 99% fit envelopes across Models X, Y and Cybertruck. I also designed, waterjetted and assembled the allufix seat jig the Model Y study was measured on, and built interior compartment CAD from A2MAC1 vehicle scans against a second ergonomic study of 90 participants.

01

The problem

Interior packaging decisions get argued in millimetres, but the evidence behind them is usually a mix of legacy assumptions and one anthropometric table. The team needed a defensible answer to a simple-sounding question: for a given seat and interior configuration, which occupants actually fit, and where does the geometry start to fail them?

Answering it across three very different vehicle lines meant the measurement rig itself had to be the constant. If the jig drifted between sessions, the data was worthless.

02

Constraints

REPEATABILITY
Every jig setting had to be indexed and re-settable to the same position weeks later.
PARTICIPANT TIME
60 people, short sessions — reconfiguration had to take minutes, not hours.
FABRICATION
Built in-house on a waterjet from flat stock — no machined one-offs, no lead time.
DISCLOSURE
Method and my scope are described here; specific dimensions and findings are not published.

04

The sequence

A
Defined what a "fit" even is
Before touching CAD: which contact points, clearances and reach conditions had to be captured, and which ones the jig would need to vary independently.
B
Designed for indexed adjustment, not infinite adjustment
Discrete detented positions instead of slots and clamps. Slower to set up, far harder to get wrong — and it makes the configuration a number you can write on the datasheet.
C
Cut it myself
Nested the profiles for the waterjet, cut, deburred and assembled. Owning fabrication meant a design change was a same-day fix rather than a purchase order.
D
Built the data pipeline
200+ product geometries reduced to a comparable parameter set, then percentile envelopes generated at 90/95/99% so packaging trade-offs could be read off directly.
E
Made it re-runnable
Documented jig settings and processing steps so a new configuration could be added later without re-deriving the method.

What I'd do differently

I built the jig before I fully specified the data schema, so the first sessions produced measurements I later had to re-map by hand. Writing the output table first would have changed two details of the fixture and saved a week of cleanup.
